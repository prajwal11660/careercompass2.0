# Country-neutral career planning

Reference: [European Skills, Competences, Qualifications and Occupations (ESCO)](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en)

This project-authored guidance is country-neutral and should not be treated as a local job-market forecast. ESCO is one public reference for exploring occupations and skills; it is European-focused, so verify titles, qualifications, and demand against the user's own country's official services and current job postings.

## Transferable ways to compare roles

Compare a person's demonstrated tasks and skills with the responsibilities in several current job descriptions. Look for repeated requirements rather than overfitting to one posting. Separate foundational capabilities (such as communication, structured problem solving, and documenting work) from role-specific tools.

## Evidence before recommendations

Explain why a role might fit using resume examples. Identify missing evidence explicitly. A suggested role is a hypothesis to explore, not a guarantee of hiring, salary, or eligibility. Encourage users to check local credentials, language requirements, work authorization, and current employer postings independently.

## Adapt the learning plan

Choose a small project or work sample that demonstrates one or two high-priority skills for a target role. Make the outcome reviewable, describe limitations honestly, and ask for feedback. Adjust scope to the user's available time and prior experience.
